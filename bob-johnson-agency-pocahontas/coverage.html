Multi-Peril Crop Insurance

Revenue Protection & Yield Protection

Crop insurance policies protect you against losses severe enough to significantly drop the yield per insured unit.

Revenue Protection

Insurance coverage that provides protection against production loss or price decline or increase, or a combination of both.

Yield Protection

Insurance coverage that only provides protection against a production loss, for crops where revenue protection is available but was not elected.

Example only

To arrive at the total bushels to potentially market, multiply APH (or expected yield) by coverage level by acres planted. A farmer with a 165-bu. corn APH, 1,000 acres and 75% coverage would look to price ahead about 123,800 bu. With revenue insurance and the harvest price option, that amount can be priced with confidence, knowing it's covered even if prices rise going into harvest.

165 bu×75%×1,000 ac≈123,800 bu

Acre by acre

Standard Crop-Hail Plan

Protection against physical damage to your crops from hail or other named perils. This acre-by-acre coverage protects against isolated damage and works with every MPCI policy. It can stand alone or be written together with an MPCI policy, and it can cover up to the full value of the crop.

  • You are entitled to an indemnity when damage from an insured peril equals or exceeds 1 percent above your selected deductible.
  • The payable loss is the assessed damage plus any extra allowance that may apply.
  • A variety of coverage options and deductibles is available.

Included at no extra cost

  • Fire and lightning damage
  • Vandalism and malicious mischief
  • Fire department service charge
  • Unlimited transit
  • Premium waiver for accidental death (not available in Kansas)

Replant payment alternative

If a qualifying hail event means you have to replant, you choose between the actual cost to replant (up to 20 percent of the limit of insurance, with no reduction of coverage on the replanted crop), or replanting at your own expense and collecting payment based on the original lost crop.

The top of your crop

Production Crop-Hail Plan

Based on your actual production history multiplied by a modified yield factor, and measured by MPCI unit rather than by acre. It covers the upper portion of your crop that your MPCI policy does not protect.

Eighty-five percent of all hailstorms are capable of generating up to a 30% reduction in yield, so most MPCI plans leave you carrying most of the hail risk. Adding a conventional crop-hail policy on top of MPCI doubles coverage on the bottom of your crop, for an occurrence that happens less than 15% of the time. Production Crop-Hail covers the portion that needs it most. It must be explained properly and fully understood, so ask us.

When it pays

Damage from an insured peril equals or exceeds 5 percent of your selected deductible, and the damaged MPCI unit has a production deficiency at harvest. Unit production must be quantified before indemnity payment.

How it works

After a qualifying peril, damage to the unit is assessed, but final adjustment waits until harvest. The actual production deficiency for the MPCI unit is then used to calculate the loss.

A fit if you…

look at exposure on a unit basis, know a hail-damaged crop can recover during the season, and are comfortable waiting for final unit production before payment. Not available in all states.

Hail policy provisions

How a 30% loss provision works

No loss is covered until the percentage of loss per acre exceeds 30%. The payable percentage is then the percent in excess of 30%. Once the loss exceeds 40%, an additional 2% is paid for each percent of loss above 40%, up to 55%, where this provision no longer applies.

Regular hail policies offer from 0% to 30% deductible, with fire, death benefits, replants and more. Production plan hail is based on your fields and your yields, works together with your crop insurance, and may be a great fit for you.

Don't risk a violation

Rebates are not legal on crop insurance

Buyers of MPCI policies should know about the Anti-Rebating Certification Statement on the insurance forms. The applicant and the agent both certify that they are not receiving or providing any form of rebate.

What counts as a rebate

Any benefit given as an inducement to buy insurance, including money, goods, services, discounts or credits. That also includes buying items or services from an MPCI customer at a price above market value, for example paying $5,000 for a car with a blue book value of $4,000.

Administrative fees, performance-based discounts and other payments approved by FCIC are not included. Source: USDA Risk Management Agency.
