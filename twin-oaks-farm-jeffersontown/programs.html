Riding instruction

Western & Huntseat, on a dressage foundation.

Twin Oaks Farm offers riding instruction in both Western and Huntseat disciplines, all based on a firm foundation of Dressage principles and Natural Horsemanship. We have students from young and older beginners to advanced riders interested in showing, and we try hard to tailor lessons to each student's goals and expectations.

During lessons we focus on the two or three things that matter most at the moment, so riders learn to do those things well. Driving lessons are also available with Gus, our friendly grulla pony.

Share board program

More riding, without owning a horse.

Many riders want to ride more than once a week but aren't quite ready to commit to owning a horse. Twin Oaks Share Board is for you.

We have put a lot of time and effort into training our horses, so share board is for approved riders only: students who have ridden with us long enough that we're comfortable with them riding unsupervised and keeping up the horse's training. Many riders take multiple weekly lessons with practice time afterwards to get there.

Share board includes the use of equipment and doesn't have to be one particular horse. The more horses you feel comfortable riding, the better rider you become.

Lesson policies

A few things to know.

Cancelling. Give notice by 10:00 AM the day of your lesson (6:00 PM Friday for Saturday lessons). Without notice, a $25 late-notice payment is due before your next ride.

Make-ups. Missed lessons can be made up by rescheduling within 60 days. If we have to cancel and you can't make it up, you'll get a credit.

Changes. Call or text 502-432-2623 to change a lesson time. If you don't hear back, please try again.

Arrive early. Please come 15 minutes before your lesson to get your horse ready, and check with your instructor before getting a horse out.

Family is welcome. Parents, family and friends are always welcome to watch, and to help younger students get ready. During riding time, please observe only.

Groups. Occasionally a group may have an extra rider when someone needs to make up a lesson.
