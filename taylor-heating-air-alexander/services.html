Energy tips

Small habits, lower bills.

From our technicians, season by season.

Tune up before the season starts

A tuned-up system runs at peak efficiency and doesn’t have to work as hard, which saves money.

Change your filter regularly

Skipped filter changes and maintenance are the leading cause of breakdowns and costly repairs. If you have allergies, ask about a pleated allergy filter or a media or electronic air cleaner.

Set the thermostat and leave it

Typical settings are 78° for cooling and 68° for heating. Each degree lower in summer raises cooling costs by 3–8%. Constant adjusting is inefficient, but raising it 2–3° while you’re away can cut energy use by up to 3.5% per degree.

Waking the A/C after winter

If you cut power to your central air over the winter, wait 24 hours after restoring power before running it. The compressor needs time to warm up.

Even out upstairs and downstairs

Run the fan in “on” mode while cooling to stir the air, fully open second-floor registers and close first-floor registers to about 50%.

Keep the heat out

Shade south-facing windows with awnings or trees, consider low-E glass, and ventilate the attic: attics can reach 140–150°, which makes the upstairs warmer and the A/C work harder.

Keep vents and grilles clear

Arrange furniture and drapes so supply vents and return grilles are unobstructed, keep doors and windows closed, and go easy on kitchen and bath exhaust fans.
