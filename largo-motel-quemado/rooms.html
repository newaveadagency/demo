Area info

Explore, discover & lodge.

Quemado means "burnt" in Spanish. Spanish conquistadors named it for the blackened stones that cover the ground here.

Big game hunting

Quemado is centrally located for trophy elk and deer hunting, and widely known for its trophy elk and mule deer. Book early for the season.

Quemado Lake & Overlook Trail

One of the best lakes in the state for trout fishing. The Overlook Trail leads through ponderosa pines to a rock outcrop with views of El Caso Peak, Castle Rock and the lake, and connects to trails to El Caso Lookout and Sawmill Canyon.

About 16 miles south of Quemado

The Lightning Field

Walter De Maria's 1977 installation of 400 stainless steel poles in a 1 mile by 1 kilometer grid, recognized internationally as one of the most significant artworks of the late twentieth century.

Between Quemado and Pie Town

Very Large Array (VLA)

One of the world's premier radio observatories: 27 antennas, each 25 meters across, in a Y-shaped layout on the Plains of San Agustin, fifty miles west of Socorro.

About 60 miles before Quemado, coming from Socorro

Sister property

Drop into Reserve: the Mountaineer Inn

Reserve, about an hour's drive from Quemado, is the county seat of Catron County, named after Santa Fe attorney Thomas B. Catron. In the 1860s Mexican-American settlers founded the Upper, Lower and Middle San Francisco Plazas along the San Francisco River.
