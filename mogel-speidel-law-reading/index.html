Our history

From the Baer Building to Walnut Street.

The firm traces its roots to George F. Baer, admitted to the Berks County Bar in 1868, who led the Reading Company railroad from 1901. Through Baer, Snyder & Zieber and the firms that followed, it has served Reading families and businesses for generations.

In 1980 the firm bought the former Nolan family home at 520 Walnut Street. The domed stained-glass window, three fireplaces and hand-carved woodwork are still there, and photographs of the house in the early 1900s hang in the reception area.

Common questions

Before you call.

General information only, not legal advice for your situation. For that, talk with one of our attorneys.

What happens if I die without an estate plan?

State law decides how your assets are distributed. This process, called intestate succession, can be lengthy and expensive, may not reflect your wishes, and can add stress and conflict for your family during an already difficult time.

How often should I update my estate plan?

Review it every three to five years, or whenever you have a major life event: marriage, divorce, the birth of a child, the death of a beneficiary, or a significant change in your finances.

How is child custody determined?

Custody is decided on the best interests of the child. Courts look at the child’s age, health and needs, each parent’s ability to provide a stable home, the child’s relationship with each parent, each parent’s willingness to support the child’s relationship with the other, the child’s preference if old enough, and any history of abuse or neglect.

What should I do right after an accident?

Make sure you are safe and get medical attention if needed. Report the accident to the police, collect photographs and witness details, and notify your insurer, but avoid detailed statements until you have spoken with an attorney. Keep records of all treatment and expenses.
