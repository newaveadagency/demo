Family law

We care about protecting families. That may mean negotiating a divorce agreement that considers the interests of parents and children, or it may mean adjusting an order as your family’s needs change. We are here before, during and after a divorce.

Divorce

Divorce is emotionally, mentally and financially taxing. Every relationship is different, so we take the time to understand your situation, then handle the related issues: child support, alimony, custody and the division of property.

Child custody, guardianship & visitation

When minor children are involved, each parent wants to keep an important place in the child’s life. We help set custody and visitation arrangements and modify existing court-ordered arrangements when circumstances change.

Child support

Support depends on the income and expenses of both parents, which are not always easy to establish; where needed we may work with a forensic expert. We also help modify support orders as needs change.

  • Division of property and complex assets
  • Common law marriage divorce
  • Adoptions
  • Qualified domestic relations orders
  • Protection from abuse (PFA) orders
  • Post-judgment modifications and contempt
  • Paternity
  • Prenuptial and postnuptial agreements
What is the process for filing for divorce?

It usually follows these steps:

  1. Confirm you meet Pennsylvania’s residency requirements.
  2. Determine the grounds: no-fault or fault-based.
  3. File the divorce complaint, including any requests about custody, support, alimony and property.
  4. Serve your spouse, who then has a set time to respond.
  5. Negotiate a settlement, or if issues remain, a Divorce Hearing Officer may decide them.
  6. A divorce decree is issued and the divorce is finalized, including the transfer of property and retirement accounts.
How is child custody determined?

Custody is based on the best interests of the child. Courts consider the child’s age, health and needs; each parent’s ability to provide a stable, loving home; the child’s relationship with each parent; each parent’s willingness to support the child’s relationship with the other; the child’s preference if of sufficient age and maturity; and any history of abuse or neglect. Physical custody may be shared or primary, and legal custody (major decisions such as medical and education) may be shared or sole.

What is the difference between marital and separate property?

Marital property includes assets and debts acquired during the marriage, such as income, real estate, investments and personal property. Separate property includes what one spouse owned before the marriage, inheritances, gifts to one spouse, and property made separate by a valid prenuptial or postnuptial agreement. Courts divide marital property equitably, which is not necessarily equally.

How is child support calculated?

Pennsylvania uses guidelines that consider both parents’ incomes, the number of children, the custody arrangement, and the child’s needs, including education, health care and extraordinary expenses. The guideline amount can be adjusted for deviations from the standard considerations.

Wills & estates

Request an appointment

Estate planning is about much more than a will. It sets out your wishes for your property and protects your family from unnecessary financial and emotional stress. We build plans that can adapt as your life changes, and it is never too early to begin.

Wills

A properly drafted will makes sure your property goes where you intend. We guide you through choosing guardians for minor children, distributing financial assets, property and heirlooms, and naming charities to support. Without a will, the law decides instead.

Trusts

A living trust names who receives your assets, takes effect immediately and does not go through probate as a will does. We set it up properly so the decisions you make serve you and the people you love.

Powers of attorney & guardianship

A power of attorney lets someone you trust make financial, health care or business decisions if you cannot. A guardian can be given authority to care for you if you become unable to care for yourself. We help you choose the right documents and people.

  • Wills and trusts
  • Powers of attorney and living wills
  • Estate taxation
  • Succession planning
  • Probate
  • Estate and trust administration
What documents are usually in an estate plan?

Typically a will, which directs your assets and names guardians for minor children; a trust, if appropriate; a durable power of attorney for your financial affairs; and a health care power of attorney for medical decisions if you cannot make them.

What happens if I die without an estate plan?

State law decides how your assets are distributed. This process, called intestate succession, can be lengthy and expensive, may not reflect your wishes, and can add stress and conflict for your family.

How often should I update my estate plan?

Every three to five years, or after any major life event: marriage, divorce, the birth of a child, the death of a beneficiary, or a significant change in your finances.

What does an executor or administrator do?

The executor (named in the will) or administrator (appointed when there is no will) collects and inventories the assets, pays debts and taxes, distributes what remains to the beneficiaries, files the required court documents and keeps accurate records.

How long does estate administration take?

It depends on the size and complexity of the estate and whether there are disputes. Many estates take from six months to a few years; complex estates or contested wills can take longer.

What if an estate has more debts than assets?

The estate is insolvent. The executor pays as many debts as possible in the priority order set by law, and beneficiaries may receive nothing. In most cases, family members are not responsible for paying the deceased’s debts from their own funds.

Personal injury

Request an appointment

An injury caused by someone else’s carelessness takes a physical, emotional and financial toll. We pursue fair compensation for medical expenses, lost income and pain and suffering, and in the worst cases, wrongful death. We also deal with the insurance companies, so you have legal backing on your side.

Car accidents

An attorney can take on the stress of dealing with insurers, repairs and medical bills. We negotiate settlements and, when needed, go to court. Talk to an attorney especially if the accident involved a serious injury, a death, or impairment by drugs or alcohol.

Motorcycle accidents

Even careful riders are at risk from other drivers, and the injuries are often more severe than in car accidents. We represent motorcyclists injured or killed on the road in seeking compensation for medical costs, lost income, scarring and other harm.

  • Neck and back injuries
  • Dog bites
  • Emotional distress
  • Medical malpractice
  • Premises and product liability
  • Workers’ compensation
  • Slip and fall
  • Wrongful death
What should I do immediately after an accident?

Make sure you are safe and get medical attention if needed. Report the accident to the authorities, collect photographs and witness contact information, and notify your insurer, but avoid detailed statements until you have consulted an attorney. Keep records of all treatment, expenses and communications.

How much is my case worth?

It depends on the severity of your injuries, medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering, and the effect on your daily life. Every case is different and outcomes vary widely; an attorney can review your records and the evidence to discuss your options.

How long do I have to file a claim?

The deadline, called the statute of limitations, depends on the type of claim. Missing it can mean losing your right to seek compensation, so speak with an attorney as soon as possible.

Real estate & zoning

Commercial or residential, a development project has to respect land use and zoning rules: what can be built, how large and how tall, how far from the sidewalk, how much parking. Unresolved, these issues delay projects. We represent clients before municipal boards and agencies, including variance applications, relief from restrictions, and appeals.

  • Land development
  • Rezonings
  • Variances
  • Certificates of occupancy
  • Permits
  • Special use
  • Code violations
  • Environmental regulations

Real estate law

Buyer or seller, residential or commercial, we work to keep your transaction moving and to resolve issues that could put the sale at risk: purchases and sales, property disputes, condominiums and cooperatives, landlord and tenant matters, environmental clean-up and compliance, and planning and zoning approvals.

Our clients include businesses of all sizes, cooperative and condominium boards and owners, property owners, shareholders and partners, commercial and residential landlords, management companies, tenants and individuals.

How can I find my property’s zoning classification?

Contact your local zoning office or planning department, check your municipality’s online zoning map, or look at your property deed. If the classification or its rules are unclear, a zoning attorney can help.

The township will not permit the changes I want. What now?

Talk with an attorney about what you want to accomplish and what you may need to file to seek approval, such as a variance or special exception.
