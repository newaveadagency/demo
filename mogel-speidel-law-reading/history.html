Our history 1868 to today

A practice handed down since 1868.

The firm traces its roots to George F. Baer, admitted to the Berks County Bar in 1868. Its partners have carried the practice from the age of the Reading Railroad to a 1899 house on Walnut Street, often father to son.

1868

George F. Baer joins the Berks County Bar

Admitted on January 22, 1868, Baer was the leading attorney in Reading for some 30 years. By 1873 his office stood at 518 Washington Street, still known today as the Baer Building.

1898

Baer, Snyder & Zieber

Baer’s student Jefferson Snyder became his assistant and then his partner. In 1898 the firm of Baer, Snyder and Zieber was formed with third partner Philip S. Zieber.

1902

Snyder & Zieber, then Snyder, Zieber & Snyder

When Baer became President of the Reading Company in 1901, Snyder took the lead. Snyder & Zieber was formed in 1902, and Jefferson’s son, T. Lager Snyder, later joined to form Snyder, Zieber & Snyder. Clients included the Reading Company, Reading Trust Company, Reading Hardware Company and Luden’s.

1965

Snyder, Balmer & Kershner, then Balmer, Kershner, Mogel & Speidel

The firm represented local institutions such as the Reading Transportation Company and Albright College, and its attorneys helped establish the Greater Berks Development Fund and the Berks County Industrial Development Authority. Having outgrown the Baer Building, it moved to 50 N. 5th Street in 1965.

1980

520 Walnut Street

The firm bought the former Nolan family home between 5th and Church Streets, built in 1899 by Nolan & Brothers. It has been the firm’s office ever since. Frederick R. Mogel joined the firm the same year, working alongside his father, Carl F. Mogel.

1985

Mogel, Speidel, Bobb & Kershner

The firm was incorporated with founding shareholders Carl Mogel, Harry Speidel, Donald Bobb and Edwin Kershner. It continues as a general practice serving Berks County today.

The Nolan Mansion

A stonecutter’s family home, built in 1899.

William Nolan, Sr. was born in Queen’s County, Ireland, in 1840 and began his career as a stonecutter with the Philadelphia & Reading Railway Company. His firm, later Nolan & Brothers and then Nolan Construction Company, built roads, viaducts, bridges and arches from New York City to Fairmount Park and along the Schuylkill and Susquehanna Rivers.

The land at 520 Walnut Street came into the Nolan family on June 7, 1890. The house that Nolan & Brothers built there was most likely designed by architect A. F. Smith, credited with many elegant Reading residences and churches.

The renovation for office use kept the details the builders were known for: the domed stained-glass window at the top of the center stairwell, three fireplaces, and hand-carved woodwork on the banisters, windows and doors. Photographs of the interior in the early 1900s hang in the reception area.

The neighboring Nolan house at 522 Walnut Street was torn down in 1990. Its lot is now the firm’s free off-street parking.

Founders & namesakes

The names on the door.

Bar 1868

George F. Baer

Born in Somerset County in 1842; a Civil War captain and newspaper publisher before law. Local counsel for the Philadelphia and Reading Railway, later President of the Reading Company. He helped establish Reading Hospital, Reading Trust Company and the Reading Free Library.

Bar 1875

Jefferson Snyder

Born in Exeter Township in 1848; valedictorian at Lafayette College. Studied under Baer, became his partner, and was recognized as one of the leading lawyers of the state bar.

Bar 1884

Philip S. Zieber

Born in Reading in 1861; Reading High School valedictorian in 1876 and a Lafayette College graduate. Third partner in Baer, Snyder and Zieber.

Bar 1902

T. Lager Snyder

Son of Jefferson Snyder, admitted in 1902. Well respected in the legal community, he kept his favorite rocking chair in the firm library.

Bar 1926

George B. Balmer

Muhlenberg College and Harvard Law School. Berks County Bar President 1949–50 and President of the Muhlenberg College Board of Trustees; 30 years as President of the Volunteer Firemen’s Relief Association of Reading.

Bar 1932

George A. Kershner

Lafayette College and the University of Pennsylvania Law School. Concentrated in business law; partner George Balmer called him the “best draftsman I ever knew.” He created a new form of will used in Berks County estate planning.

Bar 1944

Carl F. Mogel

Albright College and the University of Pennsylvania Law School. Berks County Bar President in 1975, he practiced law for 50 years and taught Sunday School at Atonement Lutheran Church for over 60.

Bar 1948

Harry W. Speidel

Dickinson College and The Dickinson School of Law. A World War II navigator in China, Burma and India, he retired from the Air Force Reserve as a Colonel. Practiced business, probate and real property law.

Bar 1957

Donald K. Bobb

Lafayette College and the University of Pennsylvania. Served in Korea; past president of the Berks County Bar Association and a Fellow of the American College of Trust and Estate Counsel.

Bar 1961

Edwin H. Kershner

Wesleyan University and the University of Virginia. Past president of the Berks County Bar Association; joined his father George at the firm and kept Wednesday hours at a Womelsdorf office.

Context

The Berks County legal community.

Berks County was established in 1752. Justices of the Peace were commissioned by the governor; Conrad Weiser was among the first, serving from 1752 to 1760. A Lawyers Register was kept from 1752 to 1776, and the Berks County Bar Association was formed in 1867.

By the late 1800s and early 1900s, Reading was a booming city of railway expansion. The Philadelphia and Reading Railway, established in 1833, became one of America’s first conglomerates and one of the greatest coal haulers in the East, and is still remembered as a property in Monopoly.

A long history, put to work for you.
