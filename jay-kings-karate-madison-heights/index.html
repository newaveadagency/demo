01 The dojo

One art, taught properly. Nobody sits on the bench.

Jay King's is a full-time, full-service karate dojo teaching traditional Okinawan karate and kobudo in Madison Heights, Virginia.

The head instructors are certified through Shorin-Ryu Shorinkan Karate-Do of Okinawa, Japan, the line Jay King's has followed from the start. Training blends conditioning, kata, weapons and practical application, the way the art is taught on Okinawa.

Classes are grouped by age and experience, so a 5-year-old beginner and an adult brown belt each train at the right level. Every student takes part in every class. The dojo's motto says it best: Together Everyone Achieves More.

03 The style

From Okinawa to Madison Heights.

Shorin-Ryu Shorinkan grew out of Shuri-te, the karate of Okinawa's old royal capital. Jay King's teaches it as an unbroken tradition, not a mix of styles.

Kobayashi Shorin-Ryu

“Kobayashi” means young forest. The name is written 小林 – the same characters as on the Shorinkan patch.

  1. 1300s

    Okinawa-te

    Okinawa's own fighting arts meet Chinese boxing through trade with China.

  2. 1609

    Weapons banned

    After the Satsuma invasion, Okinawans may not carry arms. Empty-hand fighting and farm-tool weapons develop in secret.

  3. 1800s

    Shuri-te

    The karate of Shuri, the royal capital, becomes the root of the Shorin-Ryu family of styles.

  4. 1901

    Into the schools

    Karate, including the Pinan kata, is introduced in Okinawan public schools.

  5. 1930

    Kobayashi-ryu

    The “young forest” style is named, setting it apart within Shorin-Ryu.

  6. 1936

    “Empty hand”

    Okinawa's leading masters agree to write karate as “empty hand”.

  7. Today

    Shorinkan in Madison Heights

    Jay King's head instructors are certified through Shorin-Ryu Shorinkan Karate-Do of Okinawa, Japan, and pass the art on at 4531 S Amherst Hwy.

04 Kobudo

Six weapons. Born on the farm and the boat.

Kobudo is the weapons side of Okinawan martial arts. Most of its tools began as everyday objects of farmers and fishermen.

Bo staff

A six-foot wooden staff, the backbone of kobudo, used at long range.

Sai truncheon

A three-pronged metal weapon, usually used in pairs, to block, strike and trap.

Tonfa handle

A wooden handle-stick, often used in pairs, spun or thrust to strike.

Kama sickle

A sickle once used to harvest grain, trained in pairs by its wooden handle.

Nunchaku flail

Two short sticks joined by cord or chain, whipped in fast flowing arcs.

Eku oar

A fisherman's boat oar, handled much like the bo, with a flat blade.

07 Questions

Good to know.

What style of karate is this?

Traditional Okinawan karate of the Shorin-Ryu Shorinkan (Kobayashi-ryu) line, together with Okinawan kobudo, the weapons side of the art. The head instructors are certified through Shorin-Ryu Shorinkan Karate-Do of Okinawa, Japan.

What ages can train?

Little Dragons is for ages 4 to 6, Kids Karate for ages 7 to 12, and the Teen / Adult class is for everyone older.

Do I need any experience?

No. Classes are grouped by age and experience level, so complete beginners start at the right place.

When are classes?

The dojo is open Monday to Friday from 3 to 9 PM and closed on weekends. Times differ by program, so call 434-528-0019 for the current schedule.

Is there a trial?

The dojo has offered a free trial week of classes. Call to ask what the current offer is.
