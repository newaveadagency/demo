Programs Jay King's Okinawan Karate Dojo

Start at 4. Keep going for life.

Three age-based programs, one traditional curriculum: Okinawan karate, kata and kobudo, with a clear rank path from white belt to black belt and beyond.

ClassesA typical classKobudo kataBelt ranksDojo rules
4–6Little Dragons

Little Dragons

A kids' martial arts program for ages 4 to 6. Classes are all-inclusive – nobody sits on the bench – and keep young students moving while they build coordination and focus.

Classes are up-tempo, mixing exercise and conditioning with basic self-defense techniques in a format built for this age.

  • Ages 4–6
  • Coordination & focus
  • Basic techniques
  • Respect & courtesy
7–12Kids Karate

Kids Karate

For ages 7 to 12. Structured classes help children develop coordination, physical fitness and mental strength, and gain valuable social skills along the way.

Through positive reinforcement students work through the kyu ranks, learning kata and self-defense, and build the confidence to stand up for themselves.

  • Ages 7–12
  • Kata & self-defense
  • Fitness & focus
  • Belt rank path
13+Teen / Adult

Teen / Adult Karate

An energizing full-body workout while you learn real technique. Traditional Okinawan karate, kata, kobudo weapons and practical self-defense, for any age and fitness level.

Parents are welcome to train too – some families come to class together.

  • Teens & adults
  • Karate & kobudo
  • Self-defense
  • Full-body conditioning

01 A typical class

What happens on the dojo floor.

Every class mixes conditioning, technique and application – physical training, kata and weapons in one program.

01

Bow in

Students bow when entering and leaving the dojo floor. Shoes off, gi clean.

02

Warm-up

Conditioning with plenty of stretching, suited to every body type.

03

Basics & kata

Stances, strikes and blocks, then the forms that carry the art.

04

Application

Self-defense with real-world use and, as students advance, kobudo weapons.

04 Dojo kun

Student rules.

The rules every Jay King's student trains by.

  1. No horseplay in the dojo.
  2. No shoes on the dojo floor at any time.
  3. Arriving more than 10 minutes late means no credit for that class.
  4. Bow before entering and leaving the dojo floor.
  5. Keep your gi clean and in good condition.
  6. Never criticize the performance of another student.
  7. Address all black belt instructors as sensei.
  8. Show mutual respect to students and instructors, inside and outside the dojo.
  9. No jewelry during class.
  10. Give 100% effort at all times.
  11. Practice good hygiene and keep a neat appearance.
  12. Do your part to keep the dojo clean and orderly.
  13. Look after your own gi and equipment.
