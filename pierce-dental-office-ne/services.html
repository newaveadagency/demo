01

Preventive care

Professional cleanings & exams

A cleaning removes plaque and tartar, which can cause gingivitis and lead to periodontal disease. Then a polish removes stain, and fluoride varnish helps keep tooth structure solid.

The check-up exam includes diagnostic x-rays to see tooth and root positions and detect decay, cysts and bone loss, an oral cancer exam of the face, neck, lips, tongue, throat and gums, a periodontal exam of the gums and bone, and a check of existing restorations for new decay.

Sealants

A plastic coating on the chewing surfaces of the back teeth, where decay happens most often. Sealants "seal out" plaque and food. Children and teenagers are obvious candidates, and adults may benefit too.

02

Restorative

Amalgam restorations

Silver fillings made from silver, tin, copper and a minimal amount of mercury. Durable, highly resistant to wear, inexpensive, widely researched and still a safe, commonly used filling material.

Resin (composite) restorations

Tooth-colored fillings of glass or quartz filler in resin. Good durability in front and back teeth and a great cosmetic choice. The cost is moderate and depends on the size of the filling.

Crowns

A crown makes a tooth stronger and improves its appearance. It can support a tooth with a large filling, attach a bridge, protect a weak tooth, restore a broken one, or cover a discolored or badly shaped tooth.

Bridges

Also called a fixed partial denture, a bridge replaces one or more missing teeth and is attached to the surrounding teeth. It can be made of gold, alloys, porcelain or a combination, and only a dentist can remove it.

Root canal therapy

Needed when the pulp inside a tooth becomes inflamed or diseased. The diseased pulp is removed, and the chamber and canals are cleaned and sealed. Left untreated, it can cause pain and swelling and the tooth may have to be removed.

03

Replacing teeth

Implants

Titanium posts placed in the upper or lower jaw that anchor replacement teeth. A popular, long-term way to replace missing teeth that blends in with your other teeth.

Dentures & partials

Removable appliances that replace missing teeth, make it easier to eat and speak, and help fill out the face and profile. Partial and complete dentures, including repair or relining of older dentures.

04

Extractions & minor oral surgery

Tooth extraction

Removing a tooth because of disease, trauma or crowding. The area is numbed first, and afterwards you get a clear aftercare routine. A small amount of bleeding is normal while the bone fills in.

Wisdom teeth

Wisdom teeth may need to come out when there is pain, infection, cysts, damage to neighboring teeth, gum disease or decay, or as part of treatment for braces. Before any decision, your mouth is examined and x-rayed, and you choose the treatment together.

05

Cosmetic & straightening

Invisalign®

A custom-made series of smooth, virtually invisible plastic aligners that gradually shift your teeth into place, based on the movements your dentist plans. No metal brackets and no wires to tighten.

Whitening

In-office (chairside) bleaching usually takes one visit, with the gums protected before the bleaching agent is applied. At-home whitening uses a peroxide gel in a mouthguard; possible side effects are sensitivity or gum irritation.

Composite bonding

Tooth-colored resin used to improve the look of teeth.

06

Gums & jaw

Treatment of gum disease

Every exam checks the gums and bone around the teeth for signs of periodontal disease, so it can be treated early.

TMJ / bruxism

Possible causes of TMJ disorders include arthritis, dislocation, injury, tooth and jaw alignment, stress and teeth grinding. The exam checks joints and muscles for tenderness, clicking or popping. Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may include jaw exercises, medication, or a night guard or bite plate to reduce clenching and grinding.
