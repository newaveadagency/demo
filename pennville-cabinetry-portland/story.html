Our story

From a garage workshop to Portland, Indiana.

“What hope there is for us lies in our nascent arts, for if we are to be remembered as more than a mass of people who lived and fought wars and died, it is for our arts that we will be remembered.” Maxwell Anderson, quoted by Mark S. Goldman

  1. 1956

    A $47 table saw

    A woodworker in Pennville, Indiana promised his wife he would build her a kitchen, if only she would let him buy a $47 table saw. His cabinet shop grew from there.

  2. Next

    The Goldman family

    The Commercial Electric Company, the Goldman family business, purchased the cabinet shop. Under Mark Goldman’s father, a Harvard MBA, the company moved from Pennville to Portland, quadrupled the square footage of the factory, added a professional marketing campaign and built a management and production team.

  3. 1990

    A new president at 24

    Mark S. Goldman first walked through the factory at the age of five and “fell in love at first sight.” In 1990 his father announced that Mark would take over as President of Pennville Custom Cabinetry.

  4. Today

    60,000 square feet

    A state-of-the-art facility with a dynamic, young management team, cabinetmakers proficient with both chisels and computer-controlled equipment, and a team that averages over 10 years of Pennville experience.

“Excellence in this industry is not simply being ‘handy.’ It is a matter of constructing the intricate cabinets, finishes, and accessories over and over, with a relentless pursuit of improvement.”

Mark S. Goldman, President

Pennville & our environment

As green as the cabinetmakers who inspired us.

For centuries cabinetmaking has been one of the greenest trades there is: wood is a renewable resource, quality pieces were made to last, makers used trees from nearby and built each piece start to finish in one place, and furniture was delivered in blankets instead of layers of packaging.

Pennville’s cabinetmakers still create virtually every wood product themselves, in one Indiana facility. In Mark Goldman’s words, “we are working towards being as green as the cabinetmakers whose work inspired us in the first place.”
