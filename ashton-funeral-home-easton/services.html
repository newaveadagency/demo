The Ashton family believes every funeral should be a unique expression and tribute to a loved one. We help families make arrangements that fit their needs and make this difficult period meaningful and affordable.

Call on us for other assistance

We can help with questions about death benefits, questions about grief and bereavement, and referrals to grief counselors or support groups. We will contact the Social Security Administration with the proof of death, can help you file life insurance claims, and if your loved one was a veteran, we will help you apply for benefits through the Veterans Administration.

In the years following a death, we offer a Community Service of Remembrance during the holidays, and in the spring we dedicate a tree as a living memorial to those who died the previous year.

A helpful checklist

When a loved one dies

There are so many things to think about at this difficult time. This step-by-step list may help – and you never have to work through it alone.

  1. Contact Ashton Funeral Home if you’d like us to take your loved one into our care.
  2. Contact your clergy person, if you haven’t already done so.
  3. Contact family members, close friends or co-workers – a few calls each can spread the news.
  4. Contact the deceased’s employer, if applicable.
  5. Arrange for the care of any dependents.
  6. Arrange for the care of pets.
  7. Gather information for the obituary: photo, age, birthplace, occupation, education, memberships, military service, immediate family.
  8. Ask close friends or family to answer the phone, greet visitors and help with household tasks. Keep a record of visitors, flowers and food for later thank-yous.
  9. If your loved one lived alone, remove valuables, perishables and trash, and lock the doors and windows.
  10. Notify fraternal, religious or civic organizations.
  11. Notify the executor of the will and the attorney regarding probate.
  12. Ask the Post Office to forward mail.
  13. Locate documents: will, birth certificate, Social Security card, marriage license, life insurance policies, military discharge papers (DD214), cemetery deed, funeral prearrangements.
  1. Meet with an attorney regarding probate.
  2. Meet with an accountant regarding estate taxes.
  3. Follow up with Social Security about benefits for a spouse or dependent children – we contact them with the proof of death.
  4. File life insurance claims. We can help you with this.
  5. Cancel medical prescriptions and unneeded services such as cable, phone and newspapers.
  6. Contact the DMV to cancel the driver’s license and transfer vehicle titles.
  7. If your loved one was a veteran, we will help you apply for VA benefits.
  8. Ask the employer about any 401(k), pension or other benefits.
  9. Check for insurance benefits through credit card or loan accounts.
  10. File remaining health insurance or Medicare claims, and notify creditors in writing.
  11. Gather certified death certificates, account statements, deeds, titles and tax returns to settle the estate.
  12. Update your own will and beneficiaries if necessary.
  13. Send thank-you cards for flowers, food, gifts and pallbearers.
The wisdom of preplanning

A loving gesture to those you leave behind

Over the years, we have found preplanning to be a sound and wise practice. It brings peace of mind to you and your family, rather than waiting until life forces stressful choices to be made quickly.

What is involved in preplanning?

It can be as simple as jotting down your song preferences, or as complete as planning and funding your entire funeral and burial. There are two parts: the details of your final service, and the financial matters.

My family knows me well. Won’t they decide well?

They may – but they will be called upon to make over fifty important decisions while suffering loss and intense emotional stress. It is a kindness to make your wishes known in advance.

How extensive should my plans be?

Preplanning is good, but only up to a certain degree. The grieving process begins with doing something for the one who has died. Make the big decisions – burial or cremation, for instance – and leave some smaller details, like the exact casket, to your family. It helps them in their grief.

Should I pre-pay my funeral?

If you can, it is good to set aside money to help your family, and it can be an important part of estate planning. We offer a variety of options and payment methods, and we deposit all prepaid funds into a burial reserve fund at a local bank.

What happens to the record of my wishes?

We keep the records of your wishes in complete confidence and safety, ready when they are needed.
