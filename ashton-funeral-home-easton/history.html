Ashton history

In businesssince 1847

The Ashton Funeral Home is one of the oldest continuously operated, family-owned funeral homes in America. Over five generations of Ashtons have served the Easton community.

1847

An undertaker and coffin maker

William Keller founds the business at Pine and Bank Streets in Easton. Frank Ashton, who works for Keller, marries Keller’s daughter and takes over running the business. Funerals are held in a church or the family’s home, and the Ashton women take an active part as shroud makers and assistants.

1887

A new office on Bushkill Street

The undertaking business moves to a new office at 313 Bushkill Street, Easton.

1890s

The horse-drawn hearse

Brothers E. Harris and Walter H. Ashton join the business. Walter purchases the horse-drawn hearse that remains with the family to the present day.

1913

Easton’s first funeral parlor

Walter Ashton opens Easton’s first funeral parlor at 31 N. 4th Street.

1921

Anna Ashton, funeral director

After Walter’s death, his widow Anna Ashton succeeds him as funeral director – one of the first female licensed funeral directors in Pennsylvania. All of their children are active in funeral service.

1935

The next generation

Fred Ashton, Sr. becomes a licensed funeral director. He and his wife, Jean Melick Ashton, continue the business at 1346 Northampton Street.

1948

Home at 14th & Northampton

Fred Ashton, Sr. moves the funeral home to its current location, the Victorian mansion at 14th and Northampton Streets. His sons, Fred Jr. and Kline Sr., join the business as young men.

1972

A family of funeral directors

Fred Ashton, Jr. retires and serves two terms as mayor of Easton. Kline Ashton, Sr. and his wife Arlene continue the business; their three children, Debra, Kline Jr. and W. Scott, all become licensed funeral directors.

2007

160 years of service

Ashton Funeral Home celebrates 160 years of service to the Easton community.

Today

The fifth generation

R. Kline Ashton, Jr., Debra Jo Ashton-Chase and W. Scott Ashton lead the funeral home, communicating the rich heritage of the past while serving the community in a contemporary and relevant way.

A family of funeral directors

The fifth generation
