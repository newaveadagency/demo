Quick check

What is your door doing?

Pick the symptom. Here is what it usually means, straight from our FAQ.

Most likely: a broken spring

Doors that will not open fully are usually the result of broken springs. Do not touch or handle the springs: they are under extreme tension and can cause severe injury. Trying to open or close a door with a broken spring can hurt you and damage the door and opener.

What to do: leave the door where it is and call. Spring replacement is generally a quick fix, and both springs are replaced together to keep the door balanced.

Most likely: the safety sensor eyes

In most cases the safety sensor eyes are misaligned. Wiggle the sensors and adjust until both lights are on and solid.

Still not closing? Safety sensor eyes and circuit boards do fail and will also stop the door from closing. That is a quick diagnosis on a service call.

Most likely: a cable slipped off the drum

A crooked door, or one that has thrown rollers out of the tracks, usually means a cable has slipped off the drum, so the springs lift the door unevenly. The spring tension must be relieved before the cable can be reset.

Caution: do not try to open or close the door. Springs and cables are under extreme tension.

Check the emergency release first

Make sure the emergency release cord is engaged. If it is, the cause is usually a broken trolley, a broken motor coupler or a worn-out gear or sprocket.

Good to know: openers are designed to move doors, not lift them. The springs do the lifting.

One remote, or all of them?

If every remote stops working at the same time, it points to a circuit board problem. If just one stops working, try a new battery first.

Still dead? Remote controls do fail and you may need a new one. We service key-less entry and remotes too.
