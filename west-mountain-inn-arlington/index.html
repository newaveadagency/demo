The Inn

Your second home in Vermont

Nestled on 150 mountainside acres overlooking the Battenkill Valley, the West Mountain Inn offers romantic, antique-filled guest rooms, mountain views, lush gardens and farm-fresh dining prepared with local, seasonal ingredients.

Our dining room, living room, tavern, library and game area give you lots of space to kick back, relax and socialize. Grab a trail map and head into the woods, visit the alpacas, or settle in by the fire.

  • 14 guest rooms in the Main Inn, plus 3 Millhouse townhouse apartments
  • Full country breakfast with every room
  • Fireplace rooms, suites and screened-in porches

Dining

A scratch kitchen, fed by our neighbors

Working directly with local, organic farms and food producers, Chef Jeff Scott prepares New England fare with the best seasonal ingredients: farm-fresh produce, free-range meats and poultry, seasonal game, Vermont artisan cheeses, home-baked breads and desserts.

Produce from Mighty Food Farm, Anjali Farm and Clear Brook Farm · cheese from Vermont Creamery, Cabot Creamery and the Vermont Butter and Cheese Company · milk, butter and cream from Wilcox Farm and Thomas Dairy. Long-time members of the Vermont Fresh Network.

Weddings & events

The Barn, built for the ages

Built by the Rochester family for their imported merino sheep and dairy, the barn still has its wide-plank oak floors, aged-cedar siding and windows on all sides. Today it hosts receptions of up to 300 guests, retreats, workshops and celebrations, and when you hold your wedding here, the whole property is yours.

Our story

Independently owned, family run

1809

A Vermont farmhouse

The West Mountain Inn is first built as a farmhouse at the foot of the mountain.

1880–1920

Mills on the Battenkill

First a gristmill and then a lumber mill operate on the river below. The mill building is today's Millhouse.

1904

A gentleman's farm

Ned and Gwendolyn Rochester buy the 150-acre farm and transform it into an elegant summer estate, with a barn for their merino sheep and dairy. Later the estate becomes an arts and retreat center.

1976

The Carlson family arrives

Visiting friends at the retreat from Hastings-on-Hudson, the Carlsons are swept off their feet by Vermont and turn the property into a country inn, complete with self-guided day trips, bike loops and hiking tours.

2000

The tradition continues

Their daughter Amie buys the inn and carries on the family tradition, welcoming guests and families who have been coming back for over 20 years.

Good to know

Policies & questions

What deposit do you require, and what is the cancellation policy?

A one night's deposit per room is required when you reserve. It is refunded, less a $20 administrative fee, only if the reservation is shortened or cancelled at least 14 days before arrival. The same policy applies when a gift certificate is used as the deposit.

Is there a minimum stay?

A 2-night minimum is required for most Saturday night stays.

Can I bring my dog?

For the health and comfort of all guests, no pets and no smoking are allowed in the inn. Guests with legitimate service animals have access to all areas of the inn.

Do you have a wheelchair-accessible room?

Yes. There is one fully wheelchair-accessible guest room on the first floor with a roll-in shower, shower chair, toilet with hand bars and roll-under sink. All first-floor public areas are wheelchair accessible, and there is an additional accessible toilet on the first floor.

What taxes and charges are added?

A 9% Vermont State Tax is added to all bills, and an 18% service charge is added to all dining and bar charges. Rates are subject to change without notice.

Can I request a specific room?

Every effort is made to honor a request for a specific room, but the inn reserves the right to use its discretion in making final room assignments.
